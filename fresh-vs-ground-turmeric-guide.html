Fresh vs Ground Turmeric

Fresh turmeric root and dried powder come from the same plant but behave differently in the kitchen. Here is how to choose, swap and store them.

  • 5 steps
  • 6 min read
  • Reviewed Nov 2026
1

How they taste

Fresh turmeric is bright, juicy and slightly peppery, with a hint of citrus and ginger. Ground turmeric is deeper, earthier and more bitter. Fresh suits pickles, juices, dressings and quick stir-fries; ground suits long-cooked curries, dals and spice blends.

2

Swapping one for the other

Use this rule of thumb.

  • 1 inch of fresh root gives about 1 tablespoon grated.
  • 1 tablespoon grated fresh equals about 1 teaspoon ground.
  • Taste as you go; strength varies between roots and brands.

Our turmeric converter on the home page does the sums for you.

3

Preparing fresh root

Choose firm, plump roots with bright orange flesh. Scrape off the thin skin with the edge of a teaspoon, then grate finely on a microplane or slice thinly. Wear gloves and use a glass or steel board, as the juice stains fingers and plastic.

4

Storing turmeric

Keep fresh root unwashed in a paper bag in the fridge for two to three weeks, or freeze it whole and grate from frozen. Store ground turmeric in an airtight jar away from light and heat. It keeps its colour for about a year but loses aroma over time.

5

Buying good turmeric

Good ground turmeric is a vivid, even yellow-orange with a clean, earthy smell. Buy small amounts from shops with a quick turnover, and check the best-before date. Whole dried turmeric pieces can also be ground at home in a spice grinder for the freshest flavour.
